About

Updated {{ site.updated }}

I am Professor of English Education at Chung-Ang University in Seoul, where I have served as Chair of the Department of English Education (2023–2025) and Director of the Digital Education Support Center (2024–2026). My research examines how technology — most recently generative AI — is reshaping second language teaching and learning, alongside long-standing interests in English-medium instruction and the language instructors use in English classrooms.

Much of my work synthesizes the field's evidence through meta-analysis, with recent articles in Review of Educational Research, Educational Research Review, Applied Linguistics, and Language Learning & Technology. Current projects build and evaluate L2 chatbots, explore no-code approaches that let teachers create their own GenAI tools, and develop GenAI-mediated activity theory as a framework for understanding teachers' changing practice.

I received my D.Phil. in Applied Linguistics and Second Language Acquisition from the University of Oxford, supervised by Prof. Ernesto Macaro, and an M.A. in Applied Linguistics from Teachers College, Columbia University. I have been a Visiting Professor at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (2025–2026) and King Mongkut's University of Technology Thonburi (2025), serve on the editorial boards of IRAL and Language Learning & Technology, and was named to the Stanford–Elsevier list of the World's Top 2% Scientists (2025 single-year list, in the Languages & Linguistics field).

Outside my work as an applied linguist, I enjoy listening to progressive metal (e.g., Dream Theater, Opeth, and Porcupine Tree), melodic death metal (e.g., Dark Tranquillity, The Halo Effect, and Insomnium), grunge (e.g., Alice in Chains, Pearl Jam, and Soundgarden), and alternative rock (e.g., Jimmy Eat World, Incubus, and Our Lady Peace). I am also a fan of thriller and horror films, especially psychological thrillers, Japanese horror, and folk horror. Beyond that, my favorite pastimes are drinking coffee, taking walks while listening to music, and napping. I have little patience for alcohol, crowded places, or discourtesy.

I welcome research collaborations and applications from graduate students who share my research interests (see below), as well as good conversations about rock music.
